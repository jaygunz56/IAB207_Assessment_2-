Acknowledgement of Country

Traditional Custodians

Every event on Triumph Motorevents takes place on Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander Country. This page explains how to acknowledge that on your event listing.

Acknowledgement vs Welcome to Country

An Acknowledgement of Country is a short statement of respect for the Traditional Custodians of the land. Anyone can give one, which is why organisers can add one to their event listing here.

A Welcome to Country is different. It can only be given by Traditional Owners of that land, so if you'd like one at your event you'll need to contact the local Traditional Owner group directly.

Finding the Traditional Custodians for your venue

  • Start with the AIATSIS Map of Indigenous Australia to get a general idea of the language and nation groups in the area.
  • The map only shows general locations, so check with the local Land Council, Traditional Owner group or your local council before naming anyone.
  • Many racetracks and venues already use an Acknowledgement of Country, so it's worth asking them what they use.
  • If you can't confirm who the Traditional Custodians are, or there is more than one group with a connection to the land, use the generic acknowledgement instead of naming a group.

Capital cities

These are the Traditional Custodians acknowledged by each capital city's council or government. They cover the city centre, so a venue out in the suburbs or a regional circuit may be on different Country. Always check for your venue.

Brisbane: Brisbane City Council acknowledges the Traditional Custodians without naming a specific group. The Turrbal and Yuggera (also spelt Jagera) peoples are both commonly acknowledged for the Brisbane area, so if you're not sure, the generic acknowledgement is the safest choice.

Canberra: the ACT Government's protocol names the Ngunnawal people. In 2023 the government also apologised to the Ngambri people for leaving them out and agreed to review the protocol, so check the current ACT protocol before naming a group.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating it as a box to tick. An acknowledgement copied onto every listing without a second thought comes across as tokenistic. It should be sincere, and ideally reflect some effort to learn about the Country your event is on.
  • Guessing the name. Naming the wrong group, or misspelling a name, is worse than not naming anyone. If you haven't confirmed it with a Land Council, Traditional Owner group or local council, use the generic acknowledgement.
  • Assuming boundaries line up with suburbs or councils. Traditional boundaries don't follow modern maps, and the AIATSIS map only shows general areas. A circuit 30 minutes out of the city can easily be on different Country to the CBD.
  • Picking a side where Country is contested. In some places more than one group has a connection to the land. Unless you've been advised otherwise, acknowledge the Traditional Custodians without naming one group over another.
  • Calling it a Welcome to Country. Only Traditional Owners can give a Welcome. An acknowledgement on an event listing should never be described as one.
  • Copying another event's wording. An acknowledgement written for one location won't be right for a venue on different Country, so check it each time you list an event somewhere new.

Options on Triumph Motorevents

Generic

Triumph Motorevents acknowledges the Traditional Custodians of the land on which this event is held and pays respect to Elders past and present.

Enhanced

Triumph Motorevents acknowledges the [Traditional Custodians] people, the Traditional Custodians of the land on which this event is held, and pays respect to their Elders past and present.
